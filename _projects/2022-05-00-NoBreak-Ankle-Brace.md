# The Challenge
Within a collaborative team comprising both Engineering and Commerce students, we are embarking on a venture aimed at innovating a distinctive ankle brace. Recognizing a prevalent issue where individuals are reluctant to wear ankle braces until after sustaining an injury due to discomfort and performance hindrance, our objective is to create an ankle brace that delivers comparable protection to rigid counterparts during high-risk situations while offering complete flexibility during low-risk periods.

# What I learned

Working within a joint Engineering and Commerce team through UBC's new venture development program, I led the mechanical design and validation of our brace prototype. To objectively measure performance, I designed and fabricated a custom test fixture that simulated ankle range of motion under load — our design achieved over 150% improved mobility compared to rigid braces. I also ran FEA structural analysis in Ansys to verify the brace could withstand the forces of high-risk athletic scenarios.

Beyond the engineering work, I conducted customer discovery interviews with athletes and healthcare professionals to validate the problem and refine our value proposition. Applying lean startup methodology to a hardware product gave me a real appreciation for how early user research shapes design decisions before significant resources are committed.
